# PIVOTS — Six-Dimensional Model

This file is the `[definition]` block substituted into every prompt template in this
directory. Condensed from Appendix B of the paper; the behavioral and psychological
observables are what annotators and models are asked to ground their scores in.

All scores are **unidirectional**. A's score for B is not necessarily B's score for A.
Every dimension uses the same five-point bipolar scale: -2, -1, 0, +1, +2.

---

## P — Egalitarian vs. Hierarchical Power

Whether the relationship is perceived as equal or unequal in social influence,
decision-making agency, and status.

- **+2 Extreme inequality (self-dominant).** You hold absolute agency; the other party's role is reduced to compliance. Observables: dominating the space, intrusive gaze, frequent interruptions, no back-channeling; empathy deficit, other treated as an instrument. *Judge sentencing a defendant; interrogator to a captive.*
- **+1 Noticeable inequality (self-dominant).** You lead through expertise or status; the other has some autonomy but you set the agenda. Observables: agenda setting, evaluative feedback, expansive non-threatening gestures, asymmetric verbal output. *Senior surgeon to resident; manager to employee.*
- **0 Egalitarian.** Power is a shared resource; neither can impose their will. The other is perceived as a peer. *Strategic partners; co-authors; spouses; colleagues brainstorming.*
- **-1 Noticeable inequality (other-dominant).** You recognize the other's lead and adjust to their frame. Observables: frequent nodding, hedging, self-minimizing posture, seeking non-verbal confirmation; social monitoring, mild performance anxiety. *Employee to executive; student to professor.*
- **-2 Extreme inequality (other-dominant).** The other holds absolute agency over your outcomes; you cannot resist. Observables: avoidant eye contact, physical shrinking, fawning, prolonged silence; hypervigilance, learned helplessness. *Victim to oppressor.*

## I — Superficial vs. Intense Involvement

Depth of the relationship: emotional intimacy, self-disclosure, psychological interdependence.

- **+2 Very intense.** The relationship is core to self-identity; high vulnerability, private language, deep history. Observables: affective resonance, soft gaze, movement micro-sync, close proximity; merged identity ("we"), extreme empathy. *Soulmates; spouses; lifelong partners.*
- **+1 Intense.** Driven by genuine emotional connection; personal feelings shared beyond what the setting requires. Observables: frequent self-disclosure, expressive affect. *Friends; teammates of years.*
- **0 Neutral.** Standard role-based relationship: friendly and helpful, private life kept behind a social mask. Observables: polite distance, standard smiles, safe small talk. *Ordinary colleagues.*
- **-1 Superficial.** You recognize each other's face but have no bond; interaction is a quick politeness ritual. *An acquaintance; a neighbor seen a few times.*
- **-2 Very superficial.** Purely transactional; the other is a service provider or stranger. Observables: no intended eye contact, flat voice, focus only on the exchange. *A one-time cashier; asking a stranger for directions.*

## V — Positive vs. Negative Valence

Core emotional quality of the interaction, from restorative and joyful to distressing and hostile.

- **+2 Very pleasant.** Exceptionally safe, joyful, revitalizing; a sense of flow. Observables: frequent smiles, shared laughter, open posture, warm vocal resonance; high psychological safety. *A celebratory dinner with loved ones.*
- **+1 Pleasant.** Friendly, smooth, satisfying. Observables: nodding, warm smiles; feeling liked, low social tension. *A friendly catch-up with a coworker.*
- **0 Neutral.** Emotionally dry or purely factual; no significant emotion, but no tension either. *A routine status meeting; asking a stranger for the time.*
- **-1 Unpleasant.** Awkward, tense, slightly draining; an urge to escape or put up walls. Observables: forced smiles, defensive posture, sighing, brief responses. *A tense meeting about a mistake.*
- **-2 Very unpleasant.** Hostile, harmful, aggressive; triggers fight-or-flight and leaves you depleted. Observables: shouting, sneering, cold silence, aggressive gestures. *Being bullied or harassed.*

## O — Socioemotional vs. Task-Oriented Objective

Whether the interaction is driven by personal emotional needs (informal) or by the
requirements of a goal (formal). **Positive is socioemotional; negative is task-oriented.**

- **+2 Purely socioemotional.** The interaction exists for the bond alone; no external work. Observables: spontaneous dialogue, emotion, informal slang; belonging, zero pressure to perform. *Family relaxing at home; a couple on a date.*
- **+1 Tends socioemotional.** A shared context exists, but personal liking drives the interaction; people side-track into personal lives. Observables: first names, personal anecdotes, casual posture. *Colleagues you like talking with.*
- **0 Mixed.** Equal balance of goal-striving and personal trust; the task needs mutual understanding to succeed. Observables: switching between technical talk and personal check-ins. *Long-term business partners; a director and a lead actor.*
- **-1 Tends task-oriented.** Structured by a professional objective; personal talk is social lubricant. Observables: guarded politeness, agenda adherence, professional vocabulary, social-zone distance. *A new project team; a manager and a new hire.*
- **-2 Purely task-oriented.** Strictly limited to the task; individuals de-individualized into roles. Observables: scripted speech, no personal disclosure, flat tone. *A lawyer and a client in court; a pilot and air traffic control.*

## T — Temporary vs. Enduring Permanence

Expected lifespan of the relationship, from a fleeting moment to a lifelong bond.

- **+2 Very long-term / permanent.** A permanent fixture of life, deep shared history, assumed to last indefinitely (typically 5+ years). Observables: shared rituals, references to distant past and far future; high security. *A long marriage; parents and children.*
- **+1 Long-term.** Stable and expected to continue 1–5 years, with active investment in the connection. Observables: consistent communication, planning future events. *Close colleagues.*
- **0 Uncertain.** Duration unknown or in flux; a testing phase. Observables: alternating formal and informal behavior, checking in on the relationship's status. *New dating partners; temporary coworkers.*
- **-1 Short-term.** Tied to a specific project or timeframe (weeks or months); expected to fade at the deadline. *A student and a tutor for one semester.*
- **-2 One-time.** Brief, one-off, zero expectation of future contact. Observables: politeness-driven behavior, low personal eye contact. *Asking a stranger for directions.*

## S — Cooperative vs. Competitive Stance

Alignment of goals and willingness to share resources: are interests positively or
negatively linked?

- **+2 Highly cooperative.** One's success is inseparable from the other's; radical sharing of resources and information. Observables: sharing all findings, verbal and physical support; zero defensive filtering. *A championship doubles pair; a startup team.*
- **+1 Cooperative.** Willing to help and share; you keep your own toolbox but lend tools freely. Observables: offering advice, troubleshooting side-bars; mentorship mindset. *Colleagues on a project.*
- **0 Neutral / mixed.** Neither cooperative nor competitive: you don't help, but you don't hinder. Observables: minimal sharing, low information flow, own working pace. *Two strangers working in the same place.*
- **-1 Competitive.** You compare yourself to others and guard your edge. Observables: displaying best work, withholding key tips, comparing results out loud. *Salespeople on the same team.*
- **-2 Highly competitive.** Goals are mutually exclusive; information is used strategically to block the other. Observables: strategic withholding, over-guarding output; zero-sum mindset. *Lawyers in a trial.*
